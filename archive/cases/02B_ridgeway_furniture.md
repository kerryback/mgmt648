# Mini-Case 2B: Ridgeway Furniture's New Distribution Center

## Background

Ridgeway Furniture is a regional retailer considering a dedicated distribution center to serve its 40 stores. The project requires a $2 million investment in warehouse equipment at time zero, depreciated straight-line over 10 years to zero salvage value. Ridgeway's analysts have developed the following operating assumptions:

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost savings (treated as revenue) | $900,000 | $990,000 | $1,089,000 |
| Operating expenses (excl. depreciation) | $360,000 | $396,000 | $436,000 |

In addition, the distribution center will require operating net working capital equal to 15% of that year's cost savings, starting at Year 1. Ridgeway's tax rate is 30%. Assume no additional CAPEX beyond the initial investment.

## Questions

1. Construct a pro forma income statement (through EBIT) for each of the three years.

2. Compute NOPAT, add back depreciation, and subtract the change in net working capital to arrive at free cash flow for each year.

3. Ridgeway's CFO notes that net income in Year 1 is positive but free cash flow is negative. Explain why this can happen, and identify which line items are responsible.

4. Using a 10% discount rate and assuming the center operates for exactly 3 years (with working capital recovered at Year 3), calculate the project NPV. Should Ridgeway proceed?

## Key Concepts

- FCF = NOPAT + Depreciation − CAPEX − Change in Net Working Capital
- Depreciation is a non-cash expense: it reduces taxes but does not consume cash, so it is added back
- Net working capital investment is a real cash outflow not captured in accounting income
- A project can show positive net income and negative FCF simultaneously when working capital needs are large

## Solution Sketch

**Depreciation** = $2,000,000 / 10 = $200,000 per year

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue (savings) | $900,000 | $990,000 | $1,089,000 |
| Operating expenses | (360,000) | (396,000) | (436,000) |
| Depreciation | (200,000) | (200,000) | (200,000) |
| EBIT | 340,000 | 394,000 | 453,000 |
| Taxes (30%) | (102,000) | (118,200) | (135,900) |
| NOPAT | 238,000 | 275,800 | 317,100 |
| + Depreciation | 200,000 | 200,000 | 200,000 |
| − Change in NWC | (135,000) | (13,500) | (14,850) |
| FCF | 303,000 | 462,300 | 502,250 |

NWC: Year 1 = 15% × $900K = $135K; Year 2 = 15% × $990K = $148.5K; Year 3 = 15% × $1,089K = $163.35K. Change in NWC Year 1 = $135K, Year 2 = $13.5K, Year 3 = $14.85K. Recovery at end of Year 3 = +$163.35K.

Note: Year 1 FCF is positive (not negative as posited). The CFO's concern is a teaching opportunity — if NWC were larger relative to revenues (e.g., 50%), FCF could indeed go negative in Year 1 despite positive net income.
