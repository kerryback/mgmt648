# Mini-Case 2C: NovaBrew's Craft Soda Line

## Background

NovaBrew is a regional beverage company currently selling approximately 2 million cases of its flagship craft beer per year at a contribution margin of $4.00 per case. The company is evaluating a new line of premium craft sodas. To launch the soda line, NovaBrew would invest $3 million in bottling equipment today (depreciated straight-line over 5 years, $300,000 salvage value), and the project would require working capital equal to 10% of annual soda revenues.

The marketing team estimates soda revenues will be $5 million in Year 1, growing at 8% per year through Year 5. Variable costs are 55% of revenues; fixed cash operating costs are $400,000 per year. NovaBrew's tax rate is 30% and its WACC is 11%.

However, the sales team warns that the soda launch will pull some consumers away from craft beer. They estimate the soda launch will reduce beer case volume by 4% starting in Year 1.

## Questions

1. If NovaBrew ignores cannibalization, what is the Year 1 project free cash flow?

2. Incorporate the cannibalization effect. What is the true incremental free cash flow for Year 1?

3. How should NovaBrew treat the salvage value of the bottling equipment at the end of Year 5 in the FCF calculation? (Assume the tax rate applies to any gain over book value.)

4. NovaBrew's CEO argues that the cannibalization effect shouldn't matter because "we're still selling our product either way — the revenue just shifts." Evaluate this reasoning. Under what circumstances, if any, would it be correct?

## Key Concepts

- Incremental cash flows include all effects the investment has on the firm's total cash flows, not just the project's own revenues and costs
- Cannibalization of existing products is a real cash flow cost that must be included
- Salvage value at project end generates a cash inflow, but any gain above book value is taxable
- The CEO's reasoning would only be valid if beer and soda had identical contribution margins — which they do not here

## Solution Sketch

**Depreciation** = ($3,000,000 − $300,000) / 5 = $540,000 per year  
**Book value at Year 5** = $300,000 (equals salvage value, so no tax on gain)

**Year 1 — Without cannibalization:**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Revenues | $5,000,000 |
| Variable costs (55%) | (2,750,000) |
| Fixed costs | (400,000) |
| Depreciation | (540,000) |
| EBIT | 1,310,000 |
| Taxes (30%) | (393,000) |
| NOPAT | 917,000 |
| + Depreciation | 540,000 |
| − Change in NWC (10% × $5M) | (500,000) |
| FCF (no cannibalization) | $957,000 |

**Year 1 — Cannibalization effect:**

Lost beer volume = 2,000,000 × 4% = 80,000 cases  
Lost contribution = 80,000 × $4.00 = $320,000  
After-tax cost = $320,000 × (1 − 0.30) = $224,000

**True incremental FCF Year 1** = $957,000 − $224,000 = **$733,000**

**Salvage value at Year 5:**  
Book value = $300,000; salvage = $300,000; gain = $0; tax = $0  
After-tax salvage proceeds = $300,000 (added to Year 5 FCF, along with NWC recovery)
