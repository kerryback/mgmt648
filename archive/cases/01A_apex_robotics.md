# Mini-Case 1A: Apex Robotics' Autonomous Warehouse Investment

## Background

Apex Robotics is a mid-size manufacturer of industrial components with three distribution warehouses in the Southeast. The VP of Operations has proposed a $45 million investment to deploy a fully autonomous robotic picking-and-packing system across all three facilities. The system would replace approximately 200 warehouse workers over a three-year phase-in.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) labor cost savings of $8–10 million per year at full deployment, (2) error-rate reductions that the VP estimates could save another $2 million annually in returned shipments, and (3) the claim that major competitors are already planning similar upgrades.

The VP's team has prepared a detailed financial model projecting a 22% IRR. However, the model was built entirely by the team that originated the idea, and the VP's annual bonus is tied directly to cost-reduction targets.

Two board members have raised concerns. One notes that Apex's proprietary components are produced in very small batches with high SKU variety — the exact conditions under which autonomous picking systems have historically underperformed. The other notes that the vendor providing the robots is a startup with only two prior installations of this scale.

## Questions

Apply the five key issues from Chapter 1 to this investment:

1. **Does the story make sense?** What is Apex's claimed competitive or comparative advantage in undertaking this investment? Is the strategic rationale convincing? What additional information would you want?

2. **What are the risks?** Identify at least three specific risk factors. Which risks could potentially invalidate the investment thesis entirely, versus which are manageable?

3. **How can the investment be financed?** The $45 million would consume roughly 60% of Apex's available credit facility. What financing considerations are relevant here, and how might the financing choice affect the investment decision?

4. **How does the investment affect near-term earnings?** The system would be depreciated over 7 years. Labor savings phase in gradually. Sketch qualitatively how EPS might evolve over the first three years. Is this likely to be dilutive or accretive in the near term?

5. **Does the investment have inherent flexibilities?** Could this be staged? Are there follow-on opportunities if it succeeds? What happens if the vendor goes out of business mid-installation?

6. **Process question:** The VP's team built the model and the VP has a financial stake in approval. Which phase of the investment evaluation process is most critical here, and why?

## Key Concepts

- The five-issue framework is a checklist for qualitative investment assessment before (and alongside) the financial model
- "Does the story make sense?" requires honest assessment of competitive advantage — not every cost-saving investment creates sustainable value
- Staged investments reduce risk but may require upfront commitment that limits the true option value
- Decision bias is most acute when the project champion controls both the origination and the financial modeling
