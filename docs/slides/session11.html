Review

Duration

\[D = \sum_{t} t \cdot \frac{CF_t / (1+y)^t}{P}\]

The present-value-weighted average time to receipt, in years.

For a zero-coupon bond, \(D\) equals maturity. For a coupon bond, \(D\) is less than maturity.

Modified duration and the price approximation

\[D^* = \frac{D}{1+y}, \qquad \frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -D^* \times \Delta y\]

A bond with \(D = 7\) and \(y = 4\%\) has \(D^* = 6.73\). A 100 basis point rise in yields gives

\[\frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -6.73 \times 0.01 = -6.73\%\]

Immunization

Definition

A portfolio of bonds is immunized against a liability when two conditions hold at the same time:

Present value

\[PV_{\text{assets}} = PV_{\text{liability}}\]

Duration

\[D_{\text{assets}} = D_{\text{liability}}\]

Portfolio duration is the value-weighted average of the durations of the holdings.

The liability

Amount due $1,000,000
Due in 7 years
Yield curve flat at 4%
Present value $759,918
Duration 7.00 years

\[PV = \frac{1{,}000{,}000}{1.04^{7}} = 759{,}918\]

Two bonds are available

Bond Maturity Coupon Duration
A 3 years zero 3.00
B 12 years zero 12.00

Solving for the weights

Let \(w\) be the fraction in bond A:

\[3w + 12(1-w) = 7\]

\[w = \frac{12 - 7}{12 - 3} = \frac{5}{9} = 0.5556\]

The immunized portfolio

Bond Weight Amount invested Face value at maturity
A 55.56% $422,177 $474,891
B 44.44% $337,741 $540,735
Total 100% $759,918

Present value matches the liability. Duration is \(0.5556 \times 3 + 0.4444 \times 12 = 7.00\).

What the two conditions do and do not deliver

Holds

Small parallel shifts, immediately after the match is struck.

Surplus

Large parallel shifts, when asset convexity exceeds the liability’s.

Deficit

Non-parallel shifts, and the passage of time, since durations drift apart.

Rebalancing is required as time passes and as rates move.
