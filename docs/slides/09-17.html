Net PP&E, depreciation, capital expenditure

Three lines

\[\text{Net PP\&E}_t = k \times \text{Sales}_{t+1}\]

\[\text{Depreciation}_t = d \times \text{Net PP\&E}_{t-1}\]

\[\text{CapEx}_t = \text{Net PP\&E}_t - \text{Net PP\&E}_{t-1} + \text{Depreciation}_t\]

Net PP&E and the depreciation rate are assumptions. Capital expenditure is computed from them.

Capital expenditure as the residual

The asset base is the assumption

Capacity is in place before the sales it serves, so the balance at the end of year \(t\) carries year \(t+1\)’s sales.

Depreciation follows the balance

A percentage of the opening net balance, not a percentage of sales.

Capital expenditure is the residual

It funds the increase in the balance and replaces what was depreciated during the year.

The zero-growth check

If the balance never changes, capital expenditure equals depreciation.

Two points about the equity value calculation

Cash is not working capital

The 10% ratio covers receivables, inventory and payables. Cash is added separately, at face value.

Interest is not in free cash flow

The cash flows are unlevered. Debt enters through the WACC and again in the equity value calculation, never in the forecast.

Accounting

Leases: finance or operating

Does the lease hand over substantially all the risks and rewards of owning the asset? If so it is a purchase the lessor financed. Any one of five tests settles it.

  1. Ownership transfers at the end of the term
  2. There is a purchase option the lessee is reasonably certain to take
  3. The term covers a major part of the asset’s remaining economic life
  4. The payments are worth substantially all of the asset’s fair value
  5. The asset is so specialised it has no other use to the lessor

Meet none of the five and it is an operating lease.

Leases: what the choice is

Both sit on the balance sheet as a right-of-use asset and a lease liability. A finance lease splits into depreciation and interest; an operating lease is rent in EBIT.

Leave the rent in EBIT

The right-of-use asset stays out of invested capital and the lease liability out of the equity value calculation. The rent is already subtracted in EBIT.

Or capitalise the lease

Take the rent out of EBIT and depreciate the right-of-use asset in its place. The asset joins invested capital and the lease liability joins the equity value calculation.

Either is defensible. Half of the second — the lease liability subtracted in the equity value calculation, the rent still in EBIT — charges twice.

One rule

Every balance sheet item goes in exactly one of two places.

Invested capital

An item goes here if it produces the operating cash flows being discounted. The change in the item is a cash flow in the forecast.

The equity value calculation

Every other item goes here, carried at book value between enterprise value and equity value.

Putting an item in both double counts it; leaving it out of both drops it from the valuation.
