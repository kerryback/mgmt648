Net PP&E, depreciation, capital expenditure

Three lines

\[\text{Net PP\&E}_t = k \times \text{Sales}_{t+1}\]

\[\text{Depreciation}_t = d \times \text{Net PP\&E}_{t-1}\]

\[\text{CapEx}_t = \text{Net PP\&E}_t - \text{Net PP\&E}_{t-1} + \text{Depreciation}_t\]

Net PP&E and the depreciation rate are assumptions. Capital expenditure is computed from them.

Capital expenditure as the residual

The asset base is the assumption

Capacity is in place before the sales it serves, so the balance at the end of year \(t\) carries year \(t+1\)’s sales.

Depreciation follows the balance

A percentage of the opening net balance, not a percentage of sales.

Capital expenditure is the residual

It funds the increase in the balance and replaces what was depreciated during the year.

The zero-growth check

If the balance never changes, capital expenditure equals depreciation.

Two-stage growth

The first three years, and then 8%

The same company, with sales that reach 8% only in year 4: 25% growth in year 2, 15% in year 3, and a margin that climbs to 25% over the same three years. It holds 6.00 of cash and 18.00 of debt, against 4.0 million shares.

Years 1 to 3

Sales grow 25% and then 15%. The EBITDA margin climbs from 18% to 21% to 23%.

Year 4 onward

Sales grow 8% a year and the margin holds at 25%. The three ratios are fixed throughout.

Terminal value

Free cash flow in year 5

Free cash flow reaches 11.0% of sales in year 4 and stays there: the margin and all three ratios are fixed from year 4 on. Sales grow 8% in year 5 and every year after, so free cash flow does too.

Year 4 is the first year at the steady ratio. Year 5 is the first year that grows at 8%, which is why the terminal value sits at the end of year 4.

The terminal value

\[\text{TV}_4 = \frac{\text{FCF}_5}{r-g} = \frac{5.90}{0.12-0.08} = 147.55\]

Twenty-five times year-5 free cash flow, at a four-point spread between \(r\) and \(g\).

Two points about the equity value calculation

Cash is not working capital

The 10% ratio covers receivables, inventory and payables. Cash is added separately, at face value.

Interest is not in free cash flow

The cash flows are unlevered. Debt enters through the WACC and again in the equity value calculation, never in the forecast.
