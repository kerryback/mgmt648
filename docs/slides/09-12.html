Takeaways

Sunk costs

Money already spent does not enter the analysis.

Cannibalization

When a project takes revenue from an existing product, the loss is that product’s cash flow.

Allocations are averages

Charge the project what changes — which is sometimes less than the allocation and sometimes more.

Working capital is a change

You fund the increase, not the level, and you get it back at the end.

Tax schedules outlive projects

A depreciation schedule set by statute does not stop when your forecast does. The gap is what makes a disposal taxable.

Find the threshold

A breakeven turns an argument about beliefs into a question about evidence.

A Quick Case

Dunmore Brewing launches a hard seltzer at $40 million of revenue. Research says 60 percent of the volume comes out of a competitor’s seltzer, 15 percent out of Dunmore’s own light lager, and 25 percent is new to the category. The light lager carries a 44 percent contribution margin.

  • How much should Dunmore charge the seltzer project for cannibalization?
  • Should Dunmore be pleased or worried that most of the volume comes from a rival?

Another Quick Case

Ivara distributes packaged food to convenience stores from one leased warehouse. Warehouse cost is collected in a pool and charged to product lines at $14.00 per pallet shipped — $5.60 of occupancy, $8.40 of handling labor and equipment.

A proposed dry goods line would ship 40,000 pallets a year, so the model carries a $560,000 warehouse charge. The warehouse has been full for two years and the lease is fixed. The line’s inventory would go to a third-party facility, 3,000 pallet positions at $210 a position, worked by Ivara’s own crews.

  • Occupancy of the existing building: when, and how much?
  • Third-party space: when, and how much?
  • Handling labor: when, and how much?
  • What should the line be charged for warehousing?
  • Would your answer change if the warehouse had room to spare?

Takeaways

Market rates are nominal

A discount rate built from observed yields and premiums already contains expected inflation. It cannot be paired with cash flows that do not.

A flat price is a forecast

Holding a number at today’s level is a claim that the real price falls every year.

Consistency, not preference

Real and nominal both work and must agree to the dollar. Only the mixture is wrong.

Divide, do not subtract

The real rate is the nominal over inflation, minus one. The shortcut is close at 2 percent and far off at 40.

Some amounts are fixed in nominal terms

Signed contracts, statutory deductions, fixed-rate debt, dollar covenants. In a real model, each of them has to be deflated.

Inflation and the depreciation shield

Depreciation is set in the dollars of the purchase date and deducted in later, cheaper ones. Higher inflation lowers the real value of the shield, which matters most for capital-intensive projects.

Takeaways

A future choice is not a forecast

You do not forecast what you will do. You value the right to decide later, with information you do not have now.

Value at the node, then discount

Work backward. Take the maximum at each decision date, and carry that back.

Order matters

Take expectations before the decision and you have assumed the decision is made without knowing which state occurred.

Most of it is the option

The line itself barely clears. Four fifths of the staged path is the right to decide in 2028.

An option is not a fixed amount

The expansion right grows as success becomes likelier and shrinks toward nothing as it recedes. Quote it with the probability it was computed at.

When staging is worth paying for

If you are confident, build everything rather than pay to keep the decision open.

Takeaways

A point estimate is a distribution

A single column states that every input is known.

The base case is not the mean

The value at the average input is not the average value. Wherever the model multiplies uncertain things together, the two differ.

One at a time is not uncertainty

Moving each input alone holds every other input at its base value. Things that go wrong tend to go wrong together.

Correlation is a claim

It is an assertion about what drives the business, and it comes from people who know the industry, not from the model.

Pick the statistic deliberately

The mean, the median and the fifth percentile answer different questions. Report the one that matches the consequence you face.

Something chose the defaults

Every model contains assumptions nobody made on purpose.
