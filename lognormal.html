Lognormal random variables

A price cannot go negative, and a normal random variable can. The lognormal is what you get when you put the normal where it does no harm — in the exponent.

Start with a standard normal shock, ε: mean zero, standard deviation one, symmetric, unbounded in both directions. Scale and shift it to get any normal you like:

z = μ + σε     normal, mean μ, standard deviation σ

Then exponentiate. The variable x = ez is lognormal. It is positive for every value of z, however negative z gets, because the exponential of anything is positive.

x = ez   ⇔   z = log x     the same statement, written twice

Those two equations carry the same information. Exponential and logarithm undo each other, so a draw of z and a draw of x are not two events — they are one event read on two scales. "x is lognormal" and "log x is normal" say the same thing, and the second is where the name comes from.
